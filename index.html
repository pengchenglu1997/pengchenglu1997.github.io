Pengcheng Lu

I study how AI systems judge cultural works, starting with books. I am an M.S. student at the University of Washington Information School.

About

Best-seller lists, library records and reading communities like BookTok all record what readers pay attention to. Language models learn from these records and now judge books whenever someone asks what to read next. My research measures which of these signals a model can read and carry, and which are lost.

I treat measurement as the hard part. I preregister confirmatory studies, use equivalence tests before calling a result null, and check how records were built before trusting them. I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

Research

  • Which wording of a cultural signal moves a model's judgment of a book With Taixin Zhang. Controlled experiments on seven open-weight models. Under review, 2026.
  • How language models reorder the record of a platform reading culture (BookTok) Preregistered study across four model families. Under review, 2026.
  • Whether models' claims about contemporary fiction can be verified against public text With Taixin Zhang. Under review, 2026.
  • New York Times best-seller lists and public-library borrowing With Neel Gupta and Melanie Walsh. About 20 million Seattle Public Library checkouts linked to the list archive, 2008–2026. In preparation.
